# Scientific positioning

Use when the paper's contribution or overall story is genuinely unsettled, or
the author asks to compare scientific mainlines. This supplements
`argument-synthesis.md`; it does not repeat its evidence synthesis or make every
Introduction a new research-design exercise. Keep an approved question and
outline unless the author authorizes changing them.

## Start with the distinction the study actually measures

Read the authoritative definitions, analysis unit, eligible population,
comparison conditions and result version before naming the central contrast.
Translate them into scientific language without changing their meaning. For
example, two categories describing different computational roles cannot be
renamed "mere mention" and "actual implementation" if both include implemented
operations. A label hierarchy alone establishes neither quality nor maturity.

Keep current verified findings, historical findings, unresolved measurements
and proposed analyses distinct. A successful extraction or source check is not
independent validation. Conflicting author documents require a bounded question
about the affected choice; continue unaffected work without selecting authority
by timestamp. Do not quietly replace a multi-question outline with a narrower
story because that story is easier to narrate.

## Compare meaningful alternatives

When the evidence permits, develop two or three genuinely different candidate
positions. Do not manufacture alternatives or fill a quota. Distinguish a new
presentation of the same finding from a changed research question, and both
from a causal explanation requiring new evidence.

For each candidate, make these differences inspectable in the existing argument
companion or a short comparison; no separate registry or score is required:

- **Added knowledge or use:** identify who can understand, estimate, compare or
  do something more reliably than with the closest work, and the evidence that
  enables it. This can be a revised expectation, a better estimate, an identified
  boundary, a reusable resource or newly comparable evidence. Explain the gain
  from a wider scope or classification; neither dismiss these contributions nor
  treat their construction alone as sufficient. No dramatic reversal is required.
- **Closest prior work:** use the existing source comparison to identify what
  that work could already conclude and what the present evidence additionally
  distinguishes. If originals have not been checked, label novelty unresolved;
  do not create an empty literature niche or start unrelated broad retrieval.
- **Discriminating evidence:** identify the comparison that separates this
  account from its strongest competitor. Say whether it is already observed,
  still uncertain, or requires new analysis. A pattern by task does not by
  itself establish task suitability, a mechanism, or the absence of domain effects.
  For an unresolved explanation, name the smallest additional observation or
  comparison that would change the decision, and whether it can be measured
  independently of the outcome. Pass that concrete gap to method/planning work
  only when requested; proposing it does not authorize execution.
- **Defeat and remaining contribution:** specify what contrary or null result
  would weaken the proposed account, and which narrower finding would survive.
  Failure to reject an alternative is not evidence of equivalence. Do not
  promise that a preferred story will survive validation.
- **Scope and publication risk:** identify any changed population, denominator,
  measurement claim, causal strength or author-approved question. Venue fit
  depends on verified article type and actual contribution, not rhetorical style.

Recommend an option only with comparative scientific reasons and stated limits.
An author-owned change in question or central claim remains a decision for the
author. If no candidate is sufficiently supported, explain the precise missing
comparison and complete the supported part of the requested work. Do not run
new analysis, commission a model review or change a manuscript just to make a
candidate viable.

A nested definition makes the ordering of set sizes inevitable, but not the
conditional proportion, its variation or its scientific use. Check denominator,
unknown states and measurement validity before interpreting those patterns;
a higher proportion does not imply better quality or a desirable target.

## Carry the chosen question into the paper

For an accepted positioning, use the existing Introduction guidance to connect
established findings, the unresolved distinction and the study's actual answer.
Make each promised contribution identifiable in a supported Results comparison
or an explicitly prospective proposal task. Let the figure sequence expose that
same distinction when figure planning is requested. Deliver the requested prose;
the candidate comparison stays in the companion, not in the manuscript voice.
