## Establish the scientific argument

Read the relevant author-approved research question, outline, evidence and
current manuscript. Reuse existing decisions and supporting documents.
Distinguish what the literature establishes from what this study actually
measures or demonstrates. Resolve this before drafting:

- What scientific problem makes the reader need this study?
- What has the closest work already established, and under what conditions?
- What consequential question remains, and why can this study answer it?
- What is the strongest relevant alternative explanation or counterexample?

Construct a compact claim–evidence–reasoning outline: the source's actual
finding, its conditions, and why it licenses the proposed inference. The agent
does this work; missing outline or evidence-table files are not a reason to ask
the author to supply them. If the idea requires an author-owned scientific
choice, explain the alternatives and the evidence instead of silently changing it.

## Read and synthesize evidence

For literature-supported claims, read
[the existing source-grounded evidence guidance](../research-agent/references/source-grounded-synthesis.md).
Use `nature-literature-kb` for the relevant registered corpus; extend discovery when
the nearest prior work or an essential premise is missing. Read source passages
and their relevant conditions, not just retrieval hits or generated summaries.
Keep original empirical findings, reviews, perspectives and proposals distinct.

Choose references by argumentative function: foundational finding, closest
synthesis, representative demonstration, conflicting evidence or limitation.
A formal Introduction needs enough coverage to establish its territory and
niche; do not default to a handful of convenient papers. Conversely, citation
count is not a target and journal prestige is not evidence strength. Group
sources only when each supports the attached proposition.

Synthesize the relationships between studies rather than writing one summary
per paper. Explain when task, population, measurement or conditions make
findings compatible, complementary or genuinely conflicting. Do not infer
findings from titles, methods alone, or an uninspected bibliography. Missing
support requires focused reading, a narrower supported claim or an explicit
gap in the separate notes, not a hedge masking an unsupported assertion.

## Draft the requested section

For an Introduction, use the CARS logic as reasoning, not a paragraph template:
establish a consequential scientific territory through findings; make the
unresolved question emerge from those findings and the nearest prior work;
explain how the present study addresses that same question. A prevalence study
cannot answer a gap about causal mechanisms. A new classification alone does
not establish an empirical discovery. Avoid manufacturing controversy or a
literature vacuum to create drama.

Scientific storytelling is progression in understanding: each paragraph
changes what the reader knows or needs to know, and prepares the next.
Concrete cases should illuminate a general scientific relationship, not serve
as decorative anecdotes or lists of implementations. Use scientific objects,
processes, findings and their implications as the subjects of the prose.

For Results, lead with the supported comparison or finding and supply the
necessary quantitative evidence. For Discussion, explain its meaning in
relation to prior work, competing interpretations and limits. For an Abstract,
preserve the measured quantity, study design and result strength. For proposals,
describe intended work without implying completed findings. Preserve an approved
thesis structure instead of converting it to a journal article.

Respect the author's or verified venue's length and genre requirements. Without
a specified limit, allow enough space to develop the argument; do not compress
a formal Introduction into a short answer merely because chat defaults are brief.
No fixed paragraph count, citation quota, TEEL compliance or stock transition
list is required. Journal examples inform rhetorical choices, not a universal
"Nature-level" template or a claim of submission readiness.

## Review and deliver

Read the draft as an argument: does each inference follow from its evidence,
does the niche acknowledge closest prior work, and does the contribution answer
the question actually established? Check citation identity, version and source
locators, factual coverage, numerical meaning and claim strength. Preserve
negative findings and necessary uncertainty. Keep workflow status and tool
vocabulary out of scientific prose unless genuinely part of the scientific method.

Repair the specific reasoning or support defect found. Overall story rejection
calls for reconsidering the premise and synthesis, not adjective substitutions.
Language-only changes do not authorize argument reconstruction.

Assess task completion before polishing or estimating revision cost: the draft
must actually establish its question, compare the necessary prior work and make
the supported contribution legible. A short or evasive draft is not successful
because it contains few factual errors. Keep missing evidence, reasoning defects
and author style preferences distinguishable in the existing companion. Apply
the same claim/scope check across languages and across abstract, text and captions;
a good Chinese draft does not validate an English submission.

Deliver the clean requested prose with citations and a matching reference list.
For substantive section drafting/reconstruction, maintain a separate concise
companion with the argument outline, claim-to-source/page-or-section support,
material evidence limits and substantive revision rationale. Reuse existing
documents; one companion is sufficient and no schema or packet is mandatory.
If the author asks for body text only, return only that text and do not force
supporting documents into the response. Never substitute the companion for
the manuscript, or call a draft author-approved without actual acceptance.
